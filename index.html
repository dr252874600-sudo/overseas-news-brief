Detailed English brief

A New York judge on October 31 dismissed a $100 million defamation lawsuit filed by former U.S. President Donald Trump against The Wall Street Journal and its parent company, Dow Jones & Company. The lawsuit stemmed from an October 2024 article alleging that Trump's former lawyer, Michael Cohen, provided false information to the Journal in 2017 to help Trump win the newspaper's annual reporting award. Trump's team claimed the report damaged his reputation and sought $100 million in damages. However, the presiding judge ruled that Trump failed to meet the legal standard of "actual malice" required for defamation claims by public figures, which necessitates proving the defendant knew the information was false or recklessly disregarded its truth. This marks the second time Trump has attempted to sue The Wall Street Journal for defamation; a previous suit concerning an article about a birthday note to Jeffrey Epstein was also dismissed earlier in 2026. Trump's legal team has not yet commented on the latest ruling, but an appeal is anticipated. The dismissal represents another setback for Trump's efforts to use legal action against media reporting.

关注点 This ruling is significant as it reinforces the high legal bar public figures must clear to win defamation cases against media organizations in the U.S., particularly concerning allegations of "actual malice." It also represents a continued legal challenge for former President Trump in his attempts to counter critical media coverage.
