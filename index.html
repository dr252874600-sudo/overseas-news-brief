英国通胀与政府借贷创一年新高，财政大臣面临压力

Inflation and government borrowing highest for a year ahead of next week's Budget
BBC · 2026-10-21 17:45
详细中文报道

英国国家统计局（ONS）公布的数据显示，截至9月的年度通胀率从8月的3.1%加速至3.5%，这是自2025年10月以来最高水平。通胀上升的主要原因是汽油和柴油价格大幅上涨。9月份，汽油价格平均每升上涨8.2便士至169.5便士，柴油价格升至每升192.5便士，整体车用燃料价格在报告期内上涨了29.8%。ONS首席经济学家格兰特·菲茨纳指出，除了燃料价格，航空票价、电子游戏和现场活动价格的上涨也推高了通胀。 此次通胀数据对英国财政大臣约翰·希利构成重大压力，他将在下周公布其上任后的首个预算案。希利承诺将遵守工党不提高所得税、国民保险和增值税这三大主要税种的承诺，并坚持党的财政规则。然而，高企的通胀和政府借贷额给财政空间带来挑战。 数据显示，英国政府9月份的借贷额激增至近250亿英镑，使得本财年至今的政府借贷额比官方预测高出60亿英镑。这一数字反映了政府在应对经济挑战方面的财政负担加重。 汽油和柴油价格的上涨与国际地缘政治紧张局势密切相关。报告指出，中东和欧洲的冲突持续挤压全球石油供应。伊朗对霍尔木兹海峡的有效关闭，以及俄罗斯和乌克兰之间因俄乌战争而相互攻击对方的基础设施，都严重扰乱了主要的全球原油供应线和炼油能力。 此外，9月份的通胀数据也影响到福利金的计算。预计根据“三倍锁定”机制，国家养老金将最有可能上涨3.9%，与5月至7月的平均工资涨幅持平。 目前尚不清楚希利将在预算案中如何平衡财政紧缩与应对生活成本压力，以及如何应对因国际冲突导致的能源价格波动。

Detailed English brief

UK inflation accelerated to 3.5% in the year to September, the highest in a year, driven primarily by sharp increases in petrol and diesel prices. Government borrowing also surged to nearly £25bn in September, with borrowing for the financial year to date £6bn above official forecasts. This puts significant pressure on Chancellor John Healey ahead of his first Budget next week. The rise in fuel prices is linked to global oil supply disruptions stemming from conflicts in the Middle East and Europe, including Iran's effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz and attacks on infrastructure by Russia and Ukraine. Petrol prices rose to 169.5p per litre and diesel to 192.5p per litre in September. The inflation figure will also likely lead to a 3.9% increase in the state pension under the triple lock mechanism. Healey has pledged to adhere to Labour's commitment not to raise income tax, National Insurance, or VAT, and to stick to the party's fiscal rules, but the economic conditions present a challenging balancing act.

关注点 The report highlights the confluence of rising inflation and increased government borrowing in the UK, directly impacting the upcoming Budget and potentially affecting public services and household finances. The inflation surge is explicitly linked to geopolitical events in the Middle East and Europe, underscoring the global interconnectedness of energy markets and their influence on domestic economies. The Chancellor's challenge is to navigate these pressures while adhering to fiscal commitments.
