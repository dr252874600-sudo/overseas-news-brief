The Houthis inherited significant military capabilities, making their threat more durable than that of groups like ISIS or Al-Qaeda. The article contends that the international community has not treated the Houthi threat with sufficient seriousness, nor prioritized the restoration of Yemeni national institutions. Instead of viewing the Houthis as a force to be contained or negotiated with, the focus should be on addressing the root causes of the crisis by rebuilding Yemen's state apparatus.

关注点 This opinion piece offers a critical perspective on the international response to the Yemen crisis and its spillover effects on global security, particularly concerning Red Sea shipping. It argues that military solutions are insufficient and that addressing the political instability and state collapse in Yemen is paramount. This perspective challenges conventional approaches to maritime security and highlights the interconnectedness of regional conflicts with global economic stability, suggesting that a comprehensive political strategy for Yemen is essential for resolving the Red Sea security dilemma.
