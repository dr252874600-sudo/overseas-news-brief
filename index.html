According to the International Energy Agency (IEA), data centers consumed 485 terawatt-hours of electricity in 2024, equivalent to Germany's total electricity generation, accounting for 1.5% of the world's total energy supply. This figure is projected to double to 3% by 2030, with AI development being a primary driver of this exponential growth. The United Nations Economic Commission for Europe has warned that the expansion of AI hyperscalers and their data centers poses a growing threat to global electricity systems. In response to these challenges, engineers are exploring innovative solutions, such as harnessing the waste heat generated by AI data centers. The Bitten supercomputer project at the University of Southern Denmark, launched in May, is an example of this effort. It not only provides AI computing power for academic research but also aims to utilize the substantial waste heat produced during AI computations for heating purposes. This initiative seeks to demonstrate that AI computation can offer practical energy benefits to society through heat recovery, contributing to a more sustainable approach to AI development. The effectiveness and scalability of this waste heat utilization technology are subject to further evaluation.

The exploration of waste heat utilization by data centers, as demonstrated by the Danish project, represents a crucial step towards mitigating the environmental impact of AI and developing more sustainable technological infrastructure. This could influence future data center designs and energy policies globally.

The Australian Labor government has been forced to abandon its plan to impose tariffs on imported steel following strong opposition from the solar industry. The proposed tariffs were intended to protect domestic steel manufacturers but were criticized for potentially increasing the cost of solar projects. In response to industry lobbying and public pressure, the government has decided to scrap the tariff plan. A government spokesperson indicated that while protecting domestic industries is important, balancing the interests of various sectors, particularly in the context of the clean energy transition, is crucial. The decision highlights the significant influence of specific industry lobby groups on government policy. The government is now exploring alternative measures to support the domestic steel industry's competitiveness without hindering the development of key emerging industries.
