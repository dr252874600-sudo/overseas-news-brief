Detailed English brief

Brazil's fiercely contested presidential election concluded chaotically on Sunday, October 25, 2026, with polling stations forced to extend voting hours by two hours due to transportation delays and inclement weather. This extension has raised concerns about the integrity of the results. Incumbent leftist President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva accused officials in some areas of deliberately slowing public transport in regions where he had strong support in the first round, calling the situation "abominable." His right-wing opponent, Flávio Bolsonaro, also sought an extension, citing flooding that he claimed would unfairly impact his voters. The outcome of this election is significant not only for the leadership of Latin America's largest nation but also as a major test for the ascendant right-wing movement in the region. A winner is expected within hours, but the controversies surrounding the vote highlight Brazil's deep political divisions.

The outcome has significant implications for regional politics and Brazil's international relations, particularly its alignment with the US and China. Her next steps and potential presidential candidacy remain unclear.

周日（10月25日），数千名抗议者走上捷克首都布拉格的街头，反对政府提出的旨在改变公共媒体资金体系的改革计划。示威者们指责由亿万富翁总理安德烈·巴比什（Andrej Babiš）领导的执政联盟正在扼杀新闻自由。 抗议者要求撤回一项旨在废除公共广播电视的家庭许可费，并将其资金来源改为国家预算拨款的立法草案。捷克议会下院在周六（10月24日）的一次罕见通宵投票中，以快速程序批准了该法案的推进，限制了反对派的辩论时间。 该法案仍需由议会上院通过才能成为法律。反对派已承诺将该法案提交宪法法院。如果通过，该计划可能从2027年开始将公共媒体的资金削减约15%。 在布拉格老城广场的集会上，示威者高呼“受够了”、“绝不能通过”以及“4:36”，后者指的是法案在凌晨4点36分被议会批准的时间。 巴比什声称，该计划并非针对媒体独立性的攻击，而是关于资金透明度的问题。然而，批评者警告说，这一改变将允许执政联盟控制媒体，并将此与匈牙利总理欧尔班和斯洛伐克总理菲佐等民粹主义政府的做法相比较。 巴比什及其联盟的议程包括减少对乌克兰的支持，他们此前已有打压媒体的记录。公共媒体的工作人员已宣布将于11月3日举行罢工，并得到了工会、大学校长、教会和国际媒体组织的广泛支持。此外，一场更大规模的集会定于11月15日在布拉格的莱特纳公园举行，恰逢1989年推翻共产主义统治的“天鹅绒革命”纪念日前夕。

Detailed English brief

Thousands of protesters took to the streets of Prague on Sunday, October 25, 2026, to demonstrate against proposed reforms to the funding system for the Czech Republic's public media. Demonstrators accuse the government coalition, led by billionaire Prime Minister Andrej Babiš, of threatening press freedom. The protesters are demanding the withdrawal of legislation that would abolish household license fees for public radio and television and replace them with state budget allocations. The bill was fast-tracked through the lower house of parliament in a rare overnight vote on Saturday, limiting the opposition's ability to debate. If passed into law, the plan could cut public media funding by approximately 15% starting as early as next year. Prime Minister Babiš maintains the move is about funding transparency, not an attack on media independence. However, critics warn it would allow the ruling coalition to gain control over the media, drawing parallels with actions taken by populist governments in Hungary and Slovakia. Public media staff have announced a strike for November 3, with widespread support from various organizations.

关注点 The protests in Prague highlight growing concerns across Europe about government attempts to influence or control public media, often framed as funding reforms. The proposed changes in the Czech Republic, if enacted, could significantly impact media independence and freedom of the press, potentially aligning the country with other Eastern European nations where similar measures have been implemented. This event reflects a broader trend of populist governments challenging established democratic norms and institutions.

Detailed English brief

Serbia's long-serving leader, President Aleksandar Vučić, conceded defeat late on Sunday, October 25, 2026, admitting that his Serbian Progressive Party (SNS) had lost the snap parliamentary elections. Vučić, a right-wing populist who has been in power for 12 years, stated, "We still do not have final results... it is clear that we have lost these elections." The election was widely seen as a referendum on Vučić's government and the country's future direction. The snap elections were called following two years of protests triggered by a 2024 train station canopy collapse in Novi Sad, which killed 16 people and was seen as emblematic of corruption. Preliminary results indicated a record high turnout, which commentators suggested favored the student-led protest movement and its "Student List" party. This movement, composed of various professionals and lacking clear leaders, attracted anti-government voters across Serbia. Vučić, having served two presidential terms, was aiming to return as prime minister. Following his concession speech, celebrations erupted in the capital, Belgrade, with crowds dancing, chanting "victory," and setting off fireworks. "Serbia is free," one student told the BBC, calling it a "miracle."

关注点 Aleksandar Vučić's concession of defeat in Serbia marks a significant political shift after 12 years of his party's rule. The victory of the "Student List" party, a movement born from anti-corruption protests, signals a potential change in Serbia's political direction and a rejection of the established order. This outcome could influence Serbia's relationship with the EU, its stance on regional issues, and its broader democratic trajectory.
