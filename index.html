Detailed English brief

Obama criticized the current administration's decisions, particularly the war in Iran and trade tariff policies, arguing they have driven up prices and depleted US weapon stockpiles. Obama stated during a rally in Atlanta, Georgia, that the current president made a series of bad decisions, most importantly launching an unnecessary war. He referred to the strikes launched by the US and Israel on Iran on February 28, targeting Tehran and its nuclear and military facilities, which killed Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei and several high-ranking officials. Iran responded by attacking US military assets and closing the Strait of Hormuz, a vital route for global oil and LNG supplies. Despite a ceasefire in April and a memorandum of understanding in June, both have collapsed, with peace efforts stalled over Iran's nuclear program, US sanctions, and the strait's reopening. US media also reported that US weapon stocks, particularly Patriot missile interceptors, are low. Obama told supporters, "We have run down our stock of missiles, making us more vulnerable. And Iran now has veto power over the Strait of Hormuz where much of the world’s oil goes through to get to consumers." He added, "This is the reason why the price of oil is up. This is the reason why the price of diesel is up. The price of just about everything is up." He also noted, "Contrary to the promises and statements and declarations that were made seven months ago, this war is not over." Obama also criticized Trump's trade tariffs, arguing they have increased costs for American consumers rather than delivering promised economic benefits. "Economists said when you impose tariffs, consumers pay the tab," Obama said. "And that’s exactly what happened." Trump, in his Ohio rally, countered by accusing the Obama administration of economic and foreign policy failures and emphasized that his "America First" policies had brought economic growth and national security.

关注点 The exchange between Obama and Trump highlights the stark policy differences and political rhetoric shaping the US midterm elections, particularly concerning foreign policy, economic strategy, and national security. Detailed English brief

Ethiopia has received 12 Su-35 fighter jets from Russia, a move announced by Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed amid intensifying fighting in the northern Tigray region bordering Eritrea. Speaking at the Russia-Africa summit in Moscow, Abiy described the acquisition as a "milestone" that would significantly boost Ethiopia's defense capabilities. He revealed that the deal with Russia was agreed upon several years ago and the aircraft were delivered ahead of schedule. Additionally, Ethiopia plans to acquire four S-400 air defense systems from Moscow. Abiy emphasized the importance of this military hardware for protecting the country from external threats, calling Russian President Vladimir Putin a "close friend of Ethiopia." He stated that Ethiopia is now the first country in sub-Saharan Africa to possess such "advanced combat technology." "If we fail to build this capability, surviving as a country in our region will become difficult. Countries with sufficient preparation are not easily defied," he said. Russia and Ethiopia have signed several military agreements in recent years, including one in 2021 for military training and technological improvement. Abiy explained that the agreement to procure the Su-35 jets and S-400 systems was signed years ago, citing global air force trends and security threats. He noted the Su-35's capability for both air-to-air and air-to-ground combat, with the ability to detect and neutralize cruise missiles up to 200 kilometers away. Military intelligence analyst Qalaab Segatu told BBC Amharic that the acquisition indicates Ethiopia is preparing for potential wars with other countries, not just internal conflicts. He highlighted the jets' maneuverability, wide coverage, and advanced radar systems for air superiority and long-range scanning. The combination of fighter jets and air defense systems, he added, would provide a significant capability to protect major cities and critical infrastructure like the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam.

关注点 Ethiopia's acquisition of advanced Russian military hardware, particularly the Su-35 fighter jets and S-400 air defense systems, signals a significant shift in its military posture and regional security dynamics. This move, occurring amidst escalating conflict in Tigray and heightened tensions with Eritrea, suggests Ethiopia is preparing for potential wider regional conflicts. The deal underscores the deepening military ties between Ethiopia and Russia and highlights the growing influence of Russian arms in sub-Saharan Africa, potentially altering the balance of power in the Horn of Africa.

Detailed English brief

El Salvador has deployed over 2,000 soldiers to Haiti on a mission to retrieve four nationals who went missing in the Caribbean country nearly two weeks ago. Salvadoran President Nayib Bukele communicated the "robust deployment" to Haitian Prime Minister Alix Didier Fils-Aime earlier this week, according to a statement from El Salvador's foreign ministry on Wednesday. The soldiers will also assist in "security efforts against gangs in Haiti," the statement continued. Tensions between the two Central American countries have escalated over the disappearance of the four air force technicians, who were working as part of a UN-backed anti-gang organization in Haiti. The soldiers, part of the Gang Suppression Force (GSF), were ambushed in an area controlled by armed groups near the Haitian capital, Port-au-Prince, while traveling back to their base in an armored vehicle. The Salvadoran foreign ministry stated the "large-scale operation" is "supported by various countries and international organizations." Earlier this week, Haiti handed over four bodies, claiming they belonged to the missing servicemen, but President Bukele stated the corpses did not match, noting one was a child's and another wore a foreign military uniform. Bukele had threatened last week on X (formerly Twitter) to "wipe out" Haiti's gangs if the Salvadorans were not released, vowing to replicate actions taken in El Salvador. The UN Security Council had approved expanded powers for the GSF in October last year to combat worsening gang violence. However, the multinational force has struggled to penetrate gang-controlled areas. The UN reports that over 5,700 people have been killed in gang-related violence in Haiti this year, with armed gangs controlling much of the capital. Haiti has been ravaged by gang violence for years, a situation exacerbated by the political vacuum following the assassination of President Jovenel Moïse in 2021.

关注点 El Salvador's significant military deployment to Haiti underscores the severe and escalating gang violence in the Caribbean nation and the international community's growing concern. President Bukele's assertive stance, including the threat of direct intervention and the dispute over the recovered bodies, highlights the complex and volatile situation in Haiti. This event also points to the limitations of existing international efforts to stabilize Haiti and the potential for unilateral actions by concerned nations.
