澳大利亚政府目前正在对一项潜在的贸易措施进行审查，即是否延长对从越南进口的镀锌钢产品所征收的反倾销税。根据澳大利亚《1995年反倾销法》（Anti-Dumping Measures Act 1995），澳大利亚工业部（Department of Industry, Science and Resources）正在进行一项审查，以确定延长现有的反倾销税是否符合澳大利亚的国家利益。 此次审查的背景是，澳大利亚此前已对越南生产的镀锌钢征收了反倾销税，以应对可能存在的倾销行为，即出口商以低于其正常价值的价格将产品销售到澳大利亚，从而对澳大利亚国内同类产业造成实质性损害。审查将评估，如果现有措施到期后不予延长，越南镀锌钢的倾销行为是否会再次发生，以及是否会对澳大利亚的钢铁生产商造成损害。 审查过程将包括收集各方意见，包括越南出口商、澳大利亚进口商以及澳大利亚国内钢铁生产商的陈述。澳大利亚工业部将分析这些信息，并根据法律规定，评估延长反倾销税的必要性。如果审查结果表明延长税收措施符合国家利益，那么这些反倾销税将得以延续。 目前，审查的具体进展和最终决定尚未公布。越南是中国以外的另一个重要的钢铁出口国，其产品在国际市场上具有一定的竞争力。此次审查结果将对越南的镀锌钢出口商以及澳大利亚国内的钢铁行业产生直接影响。如果延长反倾销税，将有助于保护澳大利亚国内生产商免受低价进口产品的竞争压力；反之，则可能为越南产品进入澳大利亚市场提供更多机会，但也可能加剧市场竞争。

Detailed English brief

The Australian government is considering whether to extend anti-dumping duties imposed on galvanized steel products imported from Vietnam. This review, conducted under Australia's Anti-Dumping Measures Act 1995, aims to determine if extending these measures is still necessary to prevent harm to the Australian domestic industry. The review will assess whether dumping of Vietnamese galvanized steel is likely to recur and cause injury to Australian producers if the current measures expire. The process involves gathering submissions from various stakeholders, including Vietnamese exporters, Australian importers, and Australian steel manufacturers. The Department of Industry, Science and Resources will analyze this information to decide if extending the anti-dumping duties aligns with Australia's national interest. The outcome of this review will directly impact Vietnamese galvanized steel exporters and the Australian steel sector. If extended, the duties would continue to protect Australian producers from low-priced imports; otherwise, it could open more opportunities for Vietnamese products in the Australian market, potentially increasing market competition.

关注点 This potential extension of anti-dumping duties highlights ongoing trade protection measures in the steel sector and their impact on international trade flows, particularly concerning Vietnamese exports to Australia.

EU urges UK to raise tariffs on Chinese cars to avoid ‘made in Europe’ barriers

欧盟委员会正向英国政府施压，要求其提高对中国制造汽车的进口关税。此举的目的是防止中国汽车制造商利用英国相对较低的关税政策，通过在英国进行简单的组装或加工，将产品标记为“欧洲制造”，从而规避欧盟对中国进口汽车征收的更高关税。欧盟认为，这种做法可能扭曲市场竞争，损害欧洲本土汽车制造商的利益。 据报道，欧盟内部的汽车制造商对中国电动汽车的快速增长及其潜在的市场冲击表示担忧。他们认为，如果英国不提高关税，中国汽车制造商可能会将英国作为进入欧洲市场的“后门”。欧盟希望英国能够采取更严格的措施，以确保公平的贸易环境。 目前，欧盟对从中国进口的电动汽车征收17.4%至37.3%不等的关税。而英国对从中国进口的汽车征收的关税税率较低。欧盟的担忧在于，中国汽车制造商可能会利用这一差异，在英国进行低附加值的组装，然后以“欧洲制造”的身份进入欧盟市场，从而绕过欧盟的关税壁垒。 此举也可能与欧盟正在进行的对中国电动汽车反补贴调查有关。如果欧盟最终决定征收反补贴关税，那么英国的低关税政策将显得尤为突出，可能成为中国汽车进入欧洲的“漏洞”。 英国政府尚未公开回应欧盟的这一要求。英国的决定将对其中英贸易关系以及其与欧盟的未来贸易安排产生重要影响。分析人士指出，英国需要在维护自身贸易政策的独立性与避免与欧盟产生贸易摩擦之间取得平衡。

Detailed English brief

The European Commission is pressuring the UK government to increase import tariffs on Chinese-made cars. The objective is to prevent Chinese automakers from exploiting the UK's relatively lower tariff rates. By conducting simple assembly or processing in the UK, they could label their products as 'Made in Europe' and circumvent the higher tariffs the EU imposes on Chinese imports. The EU believes this practice could distort market competition and harm the interests of European car manufacturers. European automakers are reportedly concerned about the rapid growth of Chinese electric vehicles and their potential market impact. They fear that if the UK does not raise tariffs, Chinese car manufacturers might use the UK as a 'backdoor' into the European market. The EU desires stricter measures from the UK to ensure a fair trade environment. Currently, the EU imposes tariffs ranging from 17.4% to 37.3% on electric vehicles imported from China. The UK's tariff rate on cars imported from China is lower. The EU's concern is that Chinese manufacturers might exploit this difference by performing low-value-added assembly in the UK and then entering the EU market as 'Made in Europe' products, thereby bypassing EU tariff barriers. This move may also be linked to the EU's ongoing anti-subsidy investigation into Chinese electric vehicles. If the EU eventually decides to impose anti-subsidy duties, the UK's low tariff policy would become particularly conspicuous, potentially serving as a loophole for Chinese cars to enter Europe. The UK government has not publicly responded to this request from the EU. The UK's decision will significantly impact its trade relations with China and its future trade arrangements with the EU. Analysts suggest the UK needs to balance maintaining the independence of its trade policy with avoiding trade friction with the EU.

关注点 This event highlights the growing trade tensions between China, the EU, and the UK concerning the automotive sector, particularly electric vehicles. It underscores the EU's efforts to protect its domestic industries from perceived unfair competition and its willingness to use trade policy tools to achieve this. The UK's response will be crucial in shaping its post-Brexit trade relationships and its position within the global automotive supply chain.
