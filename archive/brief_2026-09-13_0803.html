“缩水通胀”现象显现：超市用廉价鱼类替代鲭鱼引担忧

'Skimpflation' warning as tinned mackerel alternative hits more supermarket shelves
BBC · 2026-09-13 04:37
详细中文报道

英国消费者组织Which?近日指出，多家英国超市正在悄然用一种名为“杰克鲭鱼”（jack mackerel）的鱼类替代传统的鲭鱼（mackerel），这种做法被认为是“缩水通胀”（skimpflation）的典型案例。据报道，为Princes等主要供应商供货的厂商已将罐头鲭鱼产品更换为杰克鲭鱼，Lidl超市也在销售该替代品。 此次更换的主要原因是北大西洋鲭鱼储量因过度捕捞而日益减少。杰克鲭鱼，尽管名字相似，但实际上是另一种鱼类，其可持续性更好，价格也比传统鲭鱼低约三分之一。然而，杰克鲭鱼的营养价值远低于传统鲭鱼，其Omega-3脂肪酸含量约为一半，维生素B12含量也仅为三分之一。 消费者组织表示担忧，认为当制造商在不明确告知的情况下更换日常食品的成分时，消费者可能在不知情的情况下支付相同价格却获得了质量和营养价值更低的产品。一些消费者已经对口味和质地的差异提出了投诉，并对标签的清晰度表示担忧。 Princes公司去年10月宣布将所有罐头鲭鱼产品更换为杰克鲭鱼，并声称该鱼类符合海洋管理委员会（MSC）的可持续性标准。该公司表示，其杰克鲭鱼产品已明确标识，并经过了广泛的产品和消费者测试，称其具有“更温和的风味和更肉质的口感”。Lidl超市则表示致力于从负责任管理的渔业采购鱼类和海产品。 Which?的食品政策主管Sue Davies强调，尽管制造商面临真实的供应链压力和可持续性挑战，但他们有责任向消费者完全透明地告知产品成分的变更。她补充说，“缩水通胀”——即悄悄改变成分以降低成本——已变得越来越普遍，但当消费者回家食用后才意识到产品已改变时，会感觉尤其“鬼祟”。

Detailed English brief

A consumer group in the UK, Which?, has warned that supermarkets are increasingly substituting traditional mackerel with a cheaper, less nutritious alternative called jack mackerel, labeling this trend "skimpflation." Major suppliers like Princes and retailers such as Lidl have switched to jack mackerel, citing dwindling stocks of Atlantic mackerel due to overfishing and the greater sustainability and lower cost of the alternative species. Jack mackerel contains significantly less omega-3 fatty acids (about half) and vitamin B12 (about one-third) compared to traditional mackerel. This substitution has led to consumer complaints about taste and texture differences, as well as concerns over the clarity of product labeling. Consumers may be paying the same price for a product with reduced nutritional quality without being fully aware of the change. Princes stated that its jack mackerel products are clearly identified and meet sustainability standards, describing the fish as having a milder flavor and meatier texture. Lidl affirmed its commitment to sourcing from responsibly managed fisheries. However, Which? emphasizes that manufacturers have a responsibility to be upfront with consumers about ingredient changes, especially when cost-cutting measures lead to a downgrade in product quality.

关注点 This report highlights the growing issue of 'skimpflation' in the food industry, where companies reduce costs by substituting ingredients, potentially impacting consumer nutrition and trust. The case of mackerel substitution raises questions about transparency in food labeling and the ethical responsibilities of manufacturers facing supply chain pressures. It reflects broader consumer concerns about value for money and product quality in an inflationary environment.
