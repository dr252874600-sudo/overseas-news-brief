乌克兰钢铁业者呼吁欧盟取消关税，称其受累于针对中国的措施

EU tariffs aimed at China hitting Ukraine, steel boss says
BBC · 2026-10-19 07:28
详细中文报道

乌克兰最大的钢铁公司之一Metinvest的首席执行官Yuriy Ryzhenkov近日向BBC表示，欧盟近期对进口钢铁施加的关税“不公平”，并且是在“未考虑乌克兰所处困境”的情况下实施的。他认为，乌克兰的钢铁产品被“捆绑”在旨在遏制来自中国等地的廉价进口商品的措施中，这对他国的生存至关重要。Ryzhenkov呼吁欧盟至少为乌克兰提供一个“例外”，允许其进入欧盟市场，因为他估计乌克兰的钢铁出口到欧盟可能会因此下降50-60%。 欧盟委员会的一位发言人回应称，欧盟在决定其钢铁政策时已“考虑到乌克兰的艰难处境”。该贸易集团近期实施了限制钢铁进口的措施，旨在保护成员国的钢铁制造商。具体措施包括将每年可免税进入欧盟的钢铁数量上限设定为1830万吨，比2024年的水平减少了47%。虽然政策中包含一些针对特定国家的配额，但总体目标是减少进口量，因为成员国难以与来自中国和土耳其等地的廉价进口商品竞争。 尽管欧盟与乌克兰之间存在自由贸易协定（FTA），但Ryzhenkov指出，FTA并不阻止欧盟实施独立的贸易保护措施。他强调，“乌克兰被捆绑在这些措施中，而没有考虑到乌克兰的处境”，并提到了俄乌战争。他认为，“进入欧洲市场对生存至关重要”，而欧盟当局制定的限制性措施“无济于事”。他认为，“乌克兰与其他所有欧盟进口商受到同等待遇是不公平的”。 欧盟表示，乌克兰的特定钢铁配额保证了金属出口，但承认该配额“低于往年水平”。发言人补充说，“尽管处于战争状态，乌克兰仍然是欧盟重要的钢铁出口国”。

Detailed English brief

Yuriy Ryzhenkov, CEO of Metinvest, one of Ukraine's largest steel companies, stated that recent EU tariffs on imported steel are "unfair" and were imposed "without any consideration for the situation Ukraine is in." He argued that Ukraine's steel products have been "bundled together" in measures aimed at curbing cheap imports from countries like China, which is crucial for the company's survival. Ryzhenkov called for the EU to provide at least a "carve out" for Ukraine to access the EU market, estimating that Ukrainian steel exports to the EU could fall by 50-60% as a result. A spokesperson for the European Commission responded that the EU "took into account the difficult situation" of Ukraine when deciding its steel policy. The bloc recently imposed restrictions on steel imports to protect its domestic manufacturers, capping tax-free steel imports at 18.3 million tonnes annually, a 47% reduction from 2024 levels. While country-specific quotas exist, the overall aim is to reduce imports due to member nations struggling to compete with cheap imports from countries like China and Turkey. Ryzhenkov pointed out that despite a free-trade agreement (FTA) between the EU and Ukraine, it does not prevent the EU from implementing separate trade protection measures. He emphasized that Ukraine has been "bundled together into those measures without any consideration for the situation Ukraine is in," referencing the war with Russia. He believes that "access to the European market is crucial for survival" and that the restrictive measures are "unhelpful." He considers it "unfair" that Ukraine is treated the same as other EU importers. The EU stated that Ukraine's specific steel quota guarantees metal exports but acknowledged it is at a "level lower than in the past years." The spokesperson added, "Despite the war effort, Ukraine remains an important exporter of steel to the EU."

关注点 This event highlights the complex geopolitical and economic challenges facing Ukraine's vital steel industry amidst the ongoing war. It underscores the tension between the EU's stated support for Ukraine and its protectionist trade policies, which can have significant unintended consequences for Ukrainian exports. The situation also points to the broader global trade dynamics where measures targeting major exporters like China can inadvertently impact other nations, particularly those in vulnerable positions.

Detailed English brief

Iron ore prices are currently testing a major support level, signaling potential for significant market volatility. Analysts at StoneX describe the current iron ore market as facing a "perfect storm," with multiple adverse factors converging to put downward pressure on prices. Specific concerns include uncertainties surrounding the global economic outlook, shifts in demand from major steel-producing nations (particularly China), and potential supply-side disruptions. Market attention is focused on the performance of China's property sector and its impact on steel demand. If economic stimulus measures in China fail to effectively boost construction and manufacturing, demand for iron ore could be suppressed. Additionally, increasing global focus on green steel production might lead some steel mills to adjust their raw material procurement strategies, potentially reducing reliance on traditional high-grade iron ore. On the supply side, while current iron ore supply appears stable, geopolitical risks, extreme weather events, and production adjustments by major mining companies (such as Vale, Rio Tinto, and BHP) could cause future disruptions. Any supply interruption could rapidly drive up prices. Analysts warn that a break below key support levels could trigger further selling and pressure the profitability of mining companies, potentially impacting related stock performance and overall commodity market sentiment. Market participants are closely monitoring price movements to assess their potential impact on steel production costs and global supply chains. Future price direction will depend on the macroeconomic environment, Chinese demand, and specific supply-side conditions.

A test of major support levels suggests a potential downturn that could impact mining company profitability, steel production costs, and the overall economic sentiment. Monitoring this event is crucial for understanding the trajectory of key industrial commodities and their influence on global markets.
