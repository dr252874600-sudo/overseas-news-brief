Detailed English brief

Coalition talks between caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud party and opposition leader Benny Gantz's National Unity party to form a unity government concluded on Sunday, November 22, without an agreement. The primary sticking points were disagreements over who would lead the next government and whether far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir would be included. Gantz's mandate to form a government expires on December 6, with a possible 14-day extension. If talks fail, Gantz may seek a confidence vote for a minority government. According to Israel's Channel 12, Likud favored Ben-Gvir's inclusion, while National Unity insisted on his exclusion. A joint statement indicated that positions were outlined and a follow-up meeting would be held, but details were scarce. Gantz's bloc secured 48 seats in the October elections, falling short of a majority. Netanyahu has advocated for a unity government for security reasons, which would allow formation without parties led by Arab citizens of Israel, who won a record 19 seats. Former Prime Minister Naftali Bennett reportedly proposed a coalition including his 'Together' list, National Unity, Likud, and Avigdor Lieberman's Yisrael Beiteinu party, with Gantz serving first as premier, followed by Netanyahu. However, Gantz opposes a rotation, and Netanyahu reportedly insists on taking the premiership first. Lieberman has ruled out joining any government with Netanyahu. Should unity talks collapse, National Unity plans to pursue a confidence vote for a minority government, relying on external support from the Arab Joint List and the United Arab List.

关注点 The failure to form a unity government in Israel highlights the deep political divisions and the challenges in establishing a stable governing coalition, particularly in the context of ongoing regional security concerns. The deadlock could lead to further political instability or a minority government reliant on potentially volatile external support, impacting Israel's domestic policy and its approach to regional challenges.

Detailed English brief

Libya's eastern government stated that an explosion on a Russian ship docked at the Benghazi naval base on Sunday, November 22, was likely caused by a drone attack. The vessel had stopped at the port for refueling and supplies while en route back to Russia. The eastern government reported no casualties, but a separate commercial ship was also targeted off the coast of Benghazi, resulting in minor injuries to its crew. The eastern government condemned the attack, calling it a violation of maritime safety and Libyan sovereignty, and stated that investigations are underway in coordination with Russian counterparts. Satellite imagery confirmed a ship on fire at the port, with high-resolution images suggesting the vessel was a Russian navy ship, the Project 20380 Steregushchiy-class corvette (Merkury), which subsequently capsized and sank. The eastern-based Government of National Stability (GNS) asserted that the Russian ship was using the port under an existing maritime agreement and emphasized Libya's non-involvement in external conflicts. Meanwhile, Libya's internationally recognized Government of National Unity (GNU) in Tripoli issued a warning against hosting foreign forces and allowing the use of facilities, suggesting such actions could have serious repercussions. Moscow has not yet commented on the incident.

关注点 This incident marks a significant escalation in the proxy conflicts potentially playing out in Libya, with a direct attack on a Russian naval vessel within a Libyan port. It raises concerns about the security of maritime traffic in the Mediterranean and the potential for wider regional involvement in Libya's ongoing instability. The differing stances of Libya's two rival governments also underscore the country's fractured political landscape.
