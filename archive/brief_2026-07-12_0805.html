Detailed English brief

Tusk was speaking on the anniversary of what Poland calls the "massacre" in Volhynia, a territory now in Ukraine where Poland claims some 100,000 ethnic Poles were killed by the Ukrainian Insurgent Army (UPA) in 1943-45. Many in Ukraine, however, view the UPA as heroes who fought for independence. Warsaw and Kyiv have long been at odds over these events, in which up to 10,000 Ukrainian civilians are also estimated to have been killed. Tusk stated, "Truth is our duty toward the victims, but also a way to overcome a painful past for the sake of a better future." He urged Ukraine to "embrace this truth" if it wishes to join the EU. This comes after Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky was stripped of Poland's highest state honour last month over naming a military unit after the UPA. Despite the historical dispute, Poland continues to support Ukraine against Russia's invasion. Zelensky acknowledged joint prayers for the victims and stated Ukraine is working to establish facts, emphasizing the current shared threat from Russia.

关注点 This event highlights the enduring historical grievances between Poland and Ukraine, which could potentially complicate their relationship even as they face a common external threat from Russia. The Polish government's emphasis on historical truth and accountability, coupled with Ukraine's complex historical narrative regarding nationalist groups, presents a delicate diplomatic challenge. The Prime Minister's call for Ukraine to "embrace this truth" as a condition for EU membership suggests that historical reconciliation may become a more prominent factor in future accession discussions.

The legislation, aimed at reducing housing costs and increasing supply, is considered the most comprehensive congressional action on the issue in the 21st century, addressing widespread public frustration with high housing expenses. Trump had previously stated he would not sign the housing bill until Congress passed voter ID legislation, a measure he has repeatedly championed based on unsubstantiated claims of election fraud. Nearly two million people have been evacuated from Zhejiang province, with schools closed and flights and train services canceled.
