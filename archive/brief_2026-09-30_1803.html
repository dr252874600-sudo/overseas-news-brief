韩国一家以习近平命名的餐厅遭中国籍人士破坏

Restaurant named after Xi Jinping attacked by Chinese nationals in South Korea
BBC · 2026-09-30 12:21
详细中文报道

韩国警方报告称，位于京畿道城南市的一家名为“习近平”的餐厅于9月24日凌晨遭到四名中国籍男子的破坏。监控录像显示，这四名蒙面男子闯入餐厅，打砸家具并喷洒油漆。据餐厅老板称，此次破坏造成的财产损失约为1500万韩元（约合11,100美元）。 餐厅老板表示，他当初取这个名字是因为想在韩国开一家“最好的中国餐厅”，并强调“没有政治意图”。然而，该餐厅已运营八年，但近一年来开始收到要求更改名称的压力。去年8月，当有消息传出习近平将出席在韩国举行的亚太经济合作组织峰会时，老板接到了数十个关于改名的电话，甚至有人提出金钱补偿。有在中国经营的企业人士曾提出巨额资金要求其更换店名，且态度坚持。 今年4月，有人就餐厅菜单上的一道名为“习近平特制米饭”的菜品向韩国知识产权局提出投诉，但该部门最终以无问题结案。 此次9月24日的袭击是老板在过去一年中经历的最严重的一次。袭击发生后，餐厅关闭了五天，并于周二重新营业。老板表示，他的团队正在考虑更换餐厅的新名称。 警方于周二表示，涉案的四名嫌疑人是中国籍男性，年龄在20至40岁之间。据信他们在袭击后已逃往中国上海和烟台。警方计划向国际刑警组织（Interpol）申请对这四人发布红色通报。红色通报并非逮捕令，而是请求世界各国警方协助拘留某人，以待引渡。 目前，尚不清楚这些袭击者是否有特定的政治动机，或者是否与近期中国国内外的政治氛围有关。警方正在进一步调查中。

Detailed English brief

A restaurant in Seongnam, South Korea, named 'Xi Jinping', was vandalized by four masked Chinese nationals in the early hours of September 24. Police reported that the suspects caused approximately 15 million won ($11,100) in damages before fleeing. Surveillance footage captured the masked men smashing furniture and spraying lacquer. The restaurant owner stated that he initially chose the name with no political intent, aiming to run the 'best Chinese restaurant' in South Korea. However, over the past year, he has received numerous demands to change the name, with some individuals offering financial incentives. In April, a complaint was filed regarding a menu item called 'Xi Jinping Special Rice', but it was dismissed. The owner described the recent attack as the most severe he has experienced. The restaurant, which had been operating for eight years, reopened after a five-day closure and is considering a new name. Police identified the suspects as Chinese men aged 20-40 and believe they fled to Shanghai and Yantai. Interpol red notices are being sought for their detention pending extradition. The motive behind the attack remains under investigation.

The demands for name changes and the subsequent vandalism suggest a coordinated effort or a strong sentiment among some Chinese nationals abroad, potentially influenced by domestic political pressures or nationalist sentiments. The police seeking Interpol red notices underscores the cross-border nature of such incidents and the challenges in managing them.
