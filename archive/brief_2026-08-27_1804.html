霍尔木兹海峡交通量锐减九成五，全球航运业受重创

How a 95 percent drop in Hormuz traffic changed global shipping
Al Jazeera · 2026-08-27 17:29
详细中文报道

美国与伊朗战争爆发六个月以来，至关重要的霍尔木兹海峡交通量急剧下降了95%，从战前每日超过100艘船锐减至仅剩5艘。这一变化对全球航运业造成了数十年来最严重的干扰。
局势概览： 霍尔木兹海峡是全球能源贸易的关键咽喉，承担着全球约三分之一的海运原油、近三分之一的液化石油气（LPG）以及相当数量的液化天然气（LNG）和成品油的运输。海峡交通量的骤降，意味着全球能源供应和商品流通受到严重阻碍。 航运业影响：根据联合国贸易和发展会议（UNCTAD）的数据，海运承担着全球约80%的贸易量。此次霍尔木兹海峡的通行受阻，对依赖海运的全球经济造成了巨大冲击。航运咨询公司Gibson Shipbrokers的董事Richard Matthews指出，霍尔木兹海峡的特殊性在于其没有可替代的海上航线，尽管存在一些管道运输，但无法弥补其在货运量上的巨大缺口。 数据分析：在战争开始前的一周，通过霍尔木兹海峡的原油平均流量约占全球总量的38%，液化石油气占29%，液化天然气占19%。战争爆发后，海湾地区的原油出口量与战前相比下降了近一半（47%）。 潜在影响：此次危机不仅影响了能源价格，也可能导致日常消费品价格上涨，并对全球供应链的稳定性构成严峻挑战。由于缺乏替代路线，航运公司不得不寻找更长、更昂贵的替代航线，增加了运输成本和时间。

Detailed English brief

Six months into the US-Iran war, traffic through the Strait of Hormuz has plummeted by 95%, from over 100 vessels daily to just five. This drastic reduction has severely disrupted global shipping, impacting the flow of oil, gas, and goods worldwide. The Strait is a critical chokepoint for global energy trade, handling approximately one-third of global seaborne crude oil and nearly one-third of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) flows, along with significant volumes of LNG and refined products. According to UNCTAD, maritime shipping accounts for about 80% of world trade by volume, making this disruption one of the worst in decades. Richard Matthews, director at Gibson Shipbrokers, highlighted that the Strait of Hormuz has no alternative maritime route, making its closure particularly significant. Before the conflict, average crude oil flows through the Strait represented about 38% of the global total, LPG 29%, and LNG 19%. Post-conflict, crude exports from the Gulf region have fallen by 47% compared to pre-war levels. The crisis poses a significant threat to global economic stability, potentially leading to increased energy prices and consumer goods costs, while forcing shipping companies to adopt longer and more expensive alternative routes.

关注点 The closure of the Strait of Hormuz, a critical global energy chokepoint, has profound implications for global trade, energy security, and economic stability. The 95% drop in traffic highlights the vulnerability of global supply chains to geopolitical events and the lack of viable alternatives for a significant portion of world trade.
