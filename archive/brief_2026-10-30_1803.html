下一步观察： 尽管供应渠道正在适应，但地缘政治风险和库存水平将继续影响油价走势。市场将密切关注战争的任何新动向以及OPEC+的产量政策。

Detailed English brief

Despite easing turmoil from the US war on Iran, oil flows out of the Gulf have increased substantially, with crude exports averaging 24.6 million barrels per day in the week to October 18, exceeding pre-war levels. However, Brent crude, the global benchmark, is trading at its highest prices since April, up more than 60 percent compared to before the war. On October 27, Brent crude front-month futures briefly topped $117 a barrel. Analysts attribute the climbing prices to anxiety about potential renewed hostilities, dwindling oil stocks, and increased transportation costs. To mitigate risks from potential Iranian attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz, shipping lines have implemented ship-to-ship transfers off Oman and the UAE. Additionally, producers are increasingly bypassing the strait via pipelines in Saudi Arabia and the UAE. Adi Imsirovic, a lecturer at the University of Oxford, stated that the market reflects expectations of continued conflict. Future price movements will depend on geopolitical developments and inventory levels.

关注点 This event highlights the ongoing impact of the US-Iran conflict on global energy markets. Despite efforts to maintain supply through alternative routes and transfer methods, geopolitical anxieties continue to drive up oil prices, affecting global economic stability and inflation.
