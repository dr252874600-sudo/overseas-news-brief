Detailed English brief

Prime Minister Péter Magyar's Tisza party utilized its two-thirds majority to pass the 17th constitutional amendment, ending Sulyok's term and also removing the head of the Constitutional Court, Péter Polt. Sulyok has five days to sign the amendment or refer it to the Constitutional Court; if he does the latter, Magyar has threatened impeachment proceedings. The opposition Fidesz party walked out before the vote, accusing the Tisza party of establishing a tyranny, while Fidesz argues the amendment grants arbitrary dismissal powers. Tisza party deputies applauded the vote's outcome. The amendment also removes judges over 70 and bars deputies who have served three terms from running again.

关注点 The removal of President Sulyok signifies a major political shift in Hungary following the recent election. It demonstrates the new government's intent to consolidate power and reshape the country's institutions, potentially altering the balance of power and the independence of judicial and executive branches. The opposition's accusations of tyranny highlight ongoing political tensions and concerns about democratic norms.
