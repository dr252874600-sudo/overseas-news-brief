Detailed English brief

Peru's Constitutional Court has overturned the 15-year prison sentence of former President Ollanta Humala and his wife, Nadine Heredia, for money laundering charges. The court ruled that the campaign contributions they received did not constitute money laundering under the law at the time. Consequently, Humala has been released from prison. The couple was convicted in 2025 for allegedly receiving illegal funds from the Venezuelan government and the Brazilian construction giant Odebrecht to finance their election campaigns in 2006 and 2011. Heredia had previously sought asylum in Brazil. Humala was the first former Peruvian president to face trial in the Odebrecht corruption scandal, which has implicated several other former leaders. His lawyer stated that the court found the acts for which he was convicted were not legally defined as money laundering. Humala proclaimed his innocence upon release, calling it political persecution. This decision nullifies the entire criminal proceeding against him.

关注点 The overturning of the conviction of a former president in a high-profile corruption case raises questions about the effectiveness of Peru's judicial system and its fight against corruption, particularly in relation to the widespread Odebrecht scandal.

The proposal, which offered $40 million to each of Fifa's 211 member associations in exchange for backing, faced widespread opposition from major football confederations, including UEFA, Concacaf, and the AFC. UEFA had threatened a boycott of the World Cup if the plans proceeded. Concacaf members reportedly rejected the proposal, with sources indicating a loss of faith in Infantino among many associations in the region. The Fifa Chief Operating Officer admitted that the organization's administration had been "deceived" about the project, and a senior advisor resigned, calling it a "bad deal for football." Infantino stated that the plan had "created divisions" and would no longer proceed. This significant backlash places considerable pressure on Infantino as he seeks re-election for a fourth term next March.
