China’s new moon mission could unlock secret of lunar ice: Why that matters
Al Jazeera · 2026-08-22 13:36
详细中文报道

中国计划在8月24日至31日期间发射“嫦娥七号”无人探测器，这是中国迄今为止最雄心勃勃的月球任务。该探测器由轨道器、着陆器、月球车和跳跃器组成，将前往月球南极的永久阴影区，那里阳光从未照射过，以寻找水冰。 自20世纪90年代以来，多项太空任务已证实月球上存在水。1969年首次阿波罗登月后，科学家曾推测月球上可能存在水，但早期样本分析显示月球干燥。2009年，美国宇航局（NASA）故意将一个火箭段及其探测器撞击月球的一个阴影陨石坑，分析扬起的尘埃，首次直接证实了月球上存在大量水冰。此后，NASA在2020年宣布，月球上的水比之前已知的更为普遍，水分子被发现封装在月球表面的矿物颗粒中，并推测更多水隐藏在永久阴影区的冰层中。 月球极地的永久阴影区是数百万甚至数十亿年来阳光未曾到达的深层陨石坑内部区域。NASA在2018年通过测绘发现了月球阴影区域的水冰，并在2020年进一步确认。了解月球水资源对于科学研究至关重要，因为古老的极地冰层可能保存了月球火山活动以及彗星和小行星撞击地球-月球系统时带来的水的记录，这可能为研究地球海洋的形成提供线索。此外，如果月球上存在足够且可实际获取的水资源，它还可以为未来的载人登月任务提供饮用水和推进剂的来源。

Detailed English brief

China is preparing to launch its Chang'e-7 unmanned probe, its most ambitious lunar mission to date, between August 24 and August 31. The mission, comprising an orbiter, lander, rover, and hopper, will target the permanently shadowed craters at the Moon's south pole to search for water ice. This region, where sunlight has not reached for millions or even billions of years, is a key focus for understanding lunar resources. Evidence for water on the Moon has accumulated over decades. While early Apollo missions returned dry samples, scientists speculated about lunar water. In 2009, NASA's Lunar Crater Observation and Sensing Satellite (LCROSS) mission directly confirmed significant water ice by analyzing the plume from a deliberate impact. In 2020, NASA announced that water molecules are more widespread than previously thought, found within mineral grains on the surface, with more ice likely hidden in permanently shadowed areas. Understanding lunar water is vital. Ancient polar ice could hold records of lunar volcanic activity and water delivered by comets and asteroids, offering clues to the formation of Earth's oceans. Accessible water resources on the Moon could also be crucial for future crewed missions, providing drinking water and propellant.

关注点 China's Chang'e-7 mission aims to significantly advance our understanding of lunar water resources, particularly in the permanently shadowed regions of the south pole. This research is critical not only for scientific insights into the Moon's history and the formation of Earth's oceans but also for the practical development of future crewed lunar missions, which could rely on in-situ resource utilization for water and fuel.
