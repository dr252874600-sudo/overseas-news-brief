Controversial Jackdaw gas field set to be approved in weeks, sources say
BBC · 2026-09-04 07:00
详细中文报道

据英国广播公司（BBC）了解，英国政府即将批准一项备受争议的北海杰克道（Jackdaw）天然气田项目。该项目的最终批准可能在九月中旬，即议会休会前。杰克道项目最初于2022年获得前保守党政府批准，但随后因环保组织成功提起法律诉讼，认为其在批准时未充分考虑气候影响而被苏格兰法院驳回。法院裁定需要进行更详细的气候评估，更新后的评估报告已于七月公布并进入公众咨询阶段。杰克道项目的所有者Adura公司（由能源巨头Shell和挪威Equinor合资成立）估计，该气田在其11年寿命期间可能产生3580万吨二氧化碳排放，相当于2023年苏格兰总排放量的90%。然而，该公司也给出了一个更可能的估计，即约2360万吨，相当于2023年苏格兰排放量的60%。项目方声称，杰克道项目在高峰期可满足英国高达6%的天然气需求，但环保组织则认为其供应量仅占英国总需求的2%。环保人士强烈反对该项目，认为在气候危机日益严峻的背景下，进一步钻探是不可接受的，并将阻碍实现减排目标。他们还认为，发展新的化石燃料项目并不能保障能源安全，反而会延缓向绿色技术的转型，对创造就业不利。该项目与此前获批的罗斯巴克（Rosebank）油田项目一样，均由Adura公司运营，并面临着类似的环保争议。

Detailed English brief

The UK government is reportedly set to approve the controversial Jackdaw North Sea gas field project within weeks, according to the BBC. The project, initially approved in 2022, was delayed by a court ruling that found its consent was given without fully considering climate impacts. Updated climate assessments have since been published and are undergoing consultation. Adura, the joint venture operating the field (a partnership between Shell and Norway's Equinor), estimates Jackdaw could supply up to 6% of the UK's peak gas demand over its 11-year lifespan. However, environmental groups contest this, suggesting it will only meet 2% of demand, and argue that approving new fossil fuel extraction projects is incompatible with climate goals and hinders the transition to green technologies. Adura estimates the project could emit between 23.6 million and 35.8 million tonnes of carbon dioxide over its lifetime. Environmentalists contend that further drilling is unconscionable given the global climate crisis and extreme weather events, and that transitioning to renewables would be more beneficial for job creation and energy security.

关注点 The potential approval of the Jackdaw gas field highlights the ongoing tension between energy security needs and climate commitments in the UK. Despite environmental concerns and legal challenges regarding climate impact assessments, the government appears poised to greenlight the project, citing its potential to meet a significant portion of the UK's gas demand. This decision, if finalized, could set a precedent for future fossil fuel developments and draw criticism from environmental groups who argue it undermines the UK's climate targets and hinders the transition to renewable energy sources. The differing estimates of the project's contribution to energy supply and its carbon footprint underscore the contentious nature of such decisions.
