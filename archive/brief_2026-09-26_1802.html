Detailed English brief

The ongoing war involving Iran has severely impacted Iraq's economy. Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi stated that the country has lost approximately $60 billion in oil revenues due to disruptions preventing about 90% of its oil exports from reaching traditional Gulf routes. Given that oil revenues constitute over 90% of Iraq's federal budget, this loss represents a devastating blow to the nation's finances. Furthermore, the conflict has disrupted global supply chains, increasing shipping times and costs for Iraqi goods. A supermarket owner in Baghdad reported that imported goods now account for only 70% of his sales, down from 90% before the war, with prices increasing by 25%-30%. He attributed these price hikes not only to shipping disruptions in the Gulf but also to significant delays in goods from China (sometimes up to three months) and higher fuel and transportation costs. Iraq is heavily reliant on imports for food, medicine, and industrial raw materials, with oil being its primary source of export revenue. The war has crippled its export capacity while simultaneously driving up import costs, plunging the nation into severe economic hardship.

关注点 This event is significant because it illustrates the far-reaching economic consequences of the Iran war, extending beyond the immediate belligerents to impact regional stability and global trade. Iraq's heavy reliance on oil exports makes it particularly vulnerable to such disruptions, highlighting the fragility of economies dependent on a single commodity and specific trade routes.

Describing the online gambling industry as a "cancer" that "either we remove the tumor, or it kills us," Lula's move comes a little over a week before the first round of voting. Recent polls show the 80-year-old incumbent in a tight race against Flávio Bolsonaro, son of former President Jair Bolsonaro. The online gambling industry was legalized in Brazil in 2018 but has since been blamed for widespread household income losses and addiction issues. Lula stated that the industry "grew without regulation under Bolsonaro's management" and highlighted the lack of restrictions preventing children and adolescents from accessing online betting. However, Flávio Bolsonaro criticized the crackdown as "populist, hypocritical, and politically motivated" during a campaign rally. The order requires congressional approval within 120 days to remain in force. While most forms of gambling remain illegal in Brazil, online betting has exploded in popularity since its legalization in 2018.

关注点 This event is significant as it demonstrates how domestic policy decisions, particularly those with social and economic implications, can become potent campaign tools in closely contested elections.
