Detailed English brief

France has temporarily banned police from using stun grenades at student protests following accusations of excessive force. The announcement came after a 15-year-old lost a hand due to a stun grenade during nationwide student demonstrations demanding more funding for education and better school conditions. The protests, which began in late September with students blocking high school entrances, have since been joined by parents, teachers, and union workers. Interior Minister Laurent Nunez stated that 18 internal investigations into alleged misconduct have been launched. The government claims the movement has been hijacked by outsiders and devolved into urban violence. According to official figures, over 6,100 people have been arrested, mostly minors, and more than 1,800 are being prosecuted. At least 715 police officers and 215 teenagers have been injured.

关注点 The French government's decision to suspend the use of stun grenades highlights growing concerns over police conduct during protests and the potential for escalation.
