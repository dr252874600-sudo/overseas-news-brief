Africa food security under threat due to Iran war, trade officials warn
Al Jazeera · 2026-10-27 05:42
详细中文报道

联合国贸易和发展会议（UNCTAD）国际贸易和商品司司长卢兹·玛丽亚·德拉·莫拉（Luz Maria de la Mora）在多哈举行的世界投资论坛上警告称，美国和以色列与伊朗的冲突正在对非洲的粮食安全构成威胁。她指出，战争导致燃料和化肥价格大幅上涨，这可能影响到非洲国家明年的作物产量和食品供应。德拉·莫拉表示，“化肥价格已大幅上涨……许多国家已决定减少化肥使用，这将影响到明年的作物产量。”她进一步警告说，“这可能会影响食品的供应和食品的价格。” 此次冲突还凸显了全球对霍尔木兹海峡的依赖性，该海峡曾是全球约五分之一石油和液化天然气贸易的通道。霍尔木兹海峡的动荡对非洲经济产生了连锁反应。津巴布韦妇女事务、社区、中小企业发展部部长莫妮卡·穆茨万瓜（Monica Mutsvangwa）表示，在津巴布韦，燃料和化肥价格上涨是一个关键问题，该国超过70%的人口依赖农业。乌干达贸易、工业和合作部部长桑杰·塔纳（Sanjay Tanna）也表示，作为另一个严重依赖农业的国家，乌干达的燃料价格已翻倍以上。 德拉·莫拉补充说，除了能源成本直接上升外，石油价格上涨还扰乱了全球供应链，其影响持续时间比预期的要长。她表示，“影响持续的时间比我们预期的或希望的要长。”此次冲击也暴露了非洲经济在应对全球经济震荡方面的脆弱性。

Detailed English brief

Officials from the United Nations Conference on Trade and Development (UNCTAD) have warned that the war between the United States and Israel against Iran is driving up fuel and fertilizer costs across Africa, threatening the region's food security. Luz Maria de la Mora, director of UNCTAD's Division on International Trade and Commodities, stated at the World Investment Forum in Doha that soaring fertilizer prices could impact crop yields and food prices next year, as many African nations are reducing fertilizer use. "Fertilizer prices have risen dramatically... many countries have decided to reduce fertilizer use, which will have an effect next year in terms of crops," she said, warning of potential impacts on food availability and prices. The conflict's disruption of the Strait of Hormuz, a vital trade route, has also affected African economies. Zimbabwe's Minister of Women Affairs, Community, Small and Medium Enterprise Development, Monica Mutsvangwa, highlighted rising fuel and fertilizer costs as a critical concern in her country, where over 70% of the population relies on agriculture. Uganda's Minister of Trade, Industry and Cooperatives, Sanjay Tanna, reported that fuel prices in his nation have more than doubled. De la Mora also noted that rising oil prices have disrupted global supply chains, with effects lasting longer than anticipated.

关注点 The conflict in the Middle East, specifically the US-Israel war on Iran, is having a significant ripple effect on global commodity prices, directly impacting food security in vulnerable African nations. This highlights the interconnectedness of geopolitical events and their economic consequences, particularly for developing economies reliant on imports and agriculture.

Detailed English brief

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva has won a fourth term as Brazil's president in a dramatic comeback, defeating right-wing challenger Jair Bolsonaro. Lula secured 51.79% of the vote, becoming the first president since Brazil's return to civilian rule in 1985 to be elected after losing the first round. He declared victory a "loss for negativity, lies" and a triumph for democracy. However, Bolsonaro has refused to recognize the result and called for it to be annulled, a move not unexpected by observers given his father's actions in previous elections. A point of contention was the Supreme Court Justice Cristiano Zanin's decision to extend voting nationwide by two hours, with Bolsonaro supporters alleging bias due to Zanin's past as Lula's lawyer. Both campaigns had requested extensions, citing issues like public transport shortages and heavy rain. The President of the Superior Electoral Court confirmed Lula's victory, stating the extension did not alter the outcome, and the significant vote margin of 4.4 million makes challenging the result difficult. Lula's campaign gained momentum after losing the first round, focusing on increasing voter turnout, which saw the largest drop in abstention between rounds since 1989. Innovative campaign strategies, including a WhatsApp channel with over 10 million supporters, are credited with boosting Lula's campaign. Despite the political victory, Brazilian financial markets reacted sharply to the unexpected election result, having anticipated a Bolsonaro win.

关注点 The re-election of Lula da Silva marks a significant political shift in Brazil, ending the dominance of the right-wing and potentially altering the country's domestic and international policy direction. The close and contested nature of the election, coupled with market volatility and Bolsonaro's refusal to concede, highlights Brazil's deep political polarization and the challenges the new administration will face in unifying the country and stabilizing the economy.
