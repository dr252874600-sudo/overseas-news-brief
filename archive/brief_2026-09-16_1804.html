Detailed English brief

Kosovo's former President Hashim Thaci and three wartime commanders are awaiting a verdict from the Kosovo war crimes tribunal on charges of war crimes and crimes against humanity. Prosecutors have requested 45-year prison sentences for each defendant, accusing them of murder, torture, and persecution during the Kosovo Liberation Army's (KLA) fight for independence from Serbia in 1998-99. Thaci and his co-defendants, former KLA commanders Jakup Krasniqi, Rexhep Selimi, and Kadri Veseli, have all pleaded not guilty. Thaci's defense team argued in February that there is no direct evidence linking him to any crimes. The case is highly contentious within Kosovo, where Thaci remains a revered figure for many ethnic Albanians who fought for independence. Tens of thousands have rallied in the capital, Pristina, calling for their acquittal. The war resulted in approximately 13,000 deaths, mostly ethnic Albanians, and displaced a million more. Kosovo declared independence in 2008, a move Belgrade does not recognize, and relations remain tense.

关注点 The verdict in the Hashim Thaci case carries significant weight for Kosovo's post-war justice and national identity. A conviction could challenge the heroic narrative of the KLA's fight for independence for many Kosovars, while an acquittal might be seen as a failure of international justice. The outcome will also impact Kosovo's ongoing efforts to normalize relations with Serbia and its aspirations for greater international integration.
