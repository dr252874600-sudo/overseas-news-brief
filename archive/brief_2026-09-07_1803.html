Detailed English brief

A new Austrian law prohibiting girls under 14 from wearing headscarves in schools has come into effect with the start of the new school year. The law bans "traditional Muslim" head coverings, such as hijabs or burqas, in all public and private schools nationwide. The government, led by the conservative People's Party (ÖVP), states the headscarf is "a sign of oppression and a means of controlling girls from the earliest childhood," and that the law is a "clear commitment to gender equality" intended to protect young girls' rights. However, critics argue the law fuels anti-Muslim sentiment and may be unconstitutional. The Islamic Community in Austria (IGGÖ) strongly opposes the ban, deeming it an unjustifiable restriction on the fundamental right to religious freedom that disproportionately affects Muslim girls. The IGGÖ believes the law risks marginalizing the very children it claims to support and will back legal challenges by girls and families who feel their fundamental rights are infringed. The Constitutional Court previously struck down a similar ban in primary schools in 2020, citing a violation of religious neutrality. Under the new regulations, teachers are expected to address violations with students and guardians. Repeated infringements could lead to notification of child services, and guardians may face fines of up to €800. Some teachers have expressed concerns about enforcing the ban. The law was introduced by a coalition government comprising the ÖVP, the Social Democrats (SPÖ), and the liberal Neos party.

关注点 This law represents a significant move by a European country to regulate religious attire in schools, sparking debate about religious freedom, secularism, and integration. The potential for legal challenges and the concerns raised by the IGGÖ highlight the divisive nature of the policy and its implications for Muslim communities in Austria.
