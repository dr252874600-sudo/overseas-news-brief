Detailed English brief

U.S. President Donald Trump has signed an executive order proposing the removal of endangered species protection for the grey wolf and its subspecies, the Mexican wolf. This move could allow ranchers to kill the canids to protect their livestock. The order stated that ranchers play a "vital role" in the food supply chain and American cultural heritage. However, conservation groups have criticized the decision, arguing it could derail long-term efforts to recover wolf populations. U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service figures show 319 Mexican wolves in the wild in the U.S. in 2025, up from just four when reintroduced in 1998. Approximately 18,000 grey wolves exist across the U.S., though they remain endangered due to habitat destruction. An earlier federal attempt to delist them in 2022 was stalled by litigation. The executive order requires Interior Secretary Doug Burgum to determine within 90 days whether grey and Mexican wolf populations have recovered sufficiently to be removed from the endangered species list. The order notes that U.S. livestock herds are at a 75-year low while beef demand has risen 10% in the past decade. Environmental groups counter that studies show wolf attacks on livestock are rare and have minimal economic impact on ranchers.

关注点 This decision highlights the ongoing tension between economic interests (ranching) and environmental conservation in the United States. It signals a potential rollback of environmental protections under the Trump administration and could lead to significant legal and ecological challenges, impacting biodiversity and the management of predator-prey relationships in the wild.
