Detailed English brief

France has recorded a significant increase in drowning deaths this summer, with 301 fatalities reported since June 19th, a 14% rise compared to the same period last year. French Minister for Sports and Youth, Marina Ferrari, attributes this "tragic toll" primarily to prolonged heatwaves gripping the country. She stated that people are taking risks by swimming in unauthorized or unsupervised areas, such as rivers and canals, and engaging in activities beyond their physical capabilities in the extreme heat. Several drownings have also occurred in private swimming pools, particularly among the elderly. The minister highlighted the danger of sudden immersion in cold water on hot days, which can cause shock, and noted that individuals may overestimate their swimming abilities. The government is collaborating with regional authorities to enhance safety measures at swimming locations. This surge in drownings occurs amidst widespread heatwaves across Europe, which have also fueled wildfires and led to temporary shutdowns of infrastructure like a nuclear power plant due to high river temperatures. Europe is warming at twice the global average rate, according to climate data.
