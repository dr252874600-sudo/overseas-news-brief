Al Jazeera · 2026-09-21 16:53

澳大利亚财政部发布的一份最新《代际报告》预测，到2065-66财年，澳大利亚的死亡人数将超过出生人数，标志着该国将进入人口负增长阶段。报告预计，未来四十年的年均人口增长率将降至0.9%，远低于过去四十年的1.4%。届时，85岁及以上的老年人口预计将增加两倍，而生育率将降至1.34。移民将成为唯一的人口增长来源。 尽管面临严峻的人口结构转变，报告预测澳大利亚经济规模到2060年代中期将翻一番以上。这一增长预期主要基于人工智能带来的生产力提升，以及女性和老年人更高的劳动力参与率。财政部长吉姆·查默斯表示，虽然人口结构变化带来了风险，但澳大利亚相比其他国家仍具有优势，但不能因此而自满。 然而，澳大利亚商业理事会（代表120多家大型企业）对政府的经济前景表示怀疑。该理事会认为，将长期生产力增长预期提高到1.2%（主要归因于人工智能）的假设“不能被视为理所当然”。报告指出，由于劳动力生产率的长期低迷，目前人均实际国内生产总值（GDP）比2023年的预测低近2000美元。澳大利亚的经济增长在很大程度上依赖于由移民驱动的人口增长，而当前正面临生活水平下降的挑战。

Detailed English brief

A new Intergenerational Report from Australia's Treasury forecasts that the country will experience more deaths than births by the 2060s, entering a phase of population decline. The report projects annual population growth to average 0.9% over the next four decades, down from 1.4% in the preceding 40 years. By 2065-66, the number of Australians aged 85 or older is expected to triple, with fertility rates falling to 1.34. Immigration is anticipated to be the sole driver of population growth. Despite these demographic shifts, the Treasury forecasts the Australian economy to more than double in size by the mid-2060s. This optimistic outlook hinges on productivity gains, particularly from artificial intelligence, and increased workforce participation by women and older Australians. Treasurer Jim Chalmers acknowledged the risks but highlighted Australia's relative advantages compared to other nations facing similar demographic challenges. However, the Business Council of Australia has expressed skepticism regarding the government's economic projections. The council questions the assumption that productivity growth will rise to 1.2% due to AI, stating it "cannot be taken for granted." The report also notes that real GDP per person is nearly $2,000 lower than projected in 2023 due to weak labor productivity growth, which has fallen to 0.8% in 2023-24 from 1.8% in 2003-04. Australia's economic growth is heavily reliant on migration-driven population increases, and the nation is currently grappling with deteriorating living standards.

关注点 Australia's demographic shift, with deaths projected to outnumber births by the 2060s, presents significant long-term challenges for its economy and social services. While the government anticipates economic growth driven by productivity and workforce participation, skepticism from business groups highlights potential risks. This demographic transition, common in developed nations, will necessitate careful policy planning regarding healthcare, pensions, immigration, and economic strategy to maintain living standards and economic vitality.
