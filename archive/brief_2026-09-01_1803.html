Rescue efforts are primarily focused on hydropower stations in Nepal, where hundreds of workers are believed to be trapped in flood-inundated tunnels. International rescue teams are involved, but progress is slow, and families of the trapped workers have expressed concerns about the pace of the operation. The disaster, which began on August 26, was triggered by a glacial collapse in the Himalayas, leading to powerful downstream floods. Scientists suggest the collapse may have involved bedrock failure beneath a glacier. Nepal's Prime Minister Balendra Shah stated that 279 workers have been rescued from hydropower projects so far, though initial estimates suggested over 900 were missing. Aid exceeding $42 million has been received, and efforts are underway to rehabilitate those who have lost homes and property.
