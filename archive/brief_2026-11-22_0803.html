Detailed English brief

On November 21, 2026, U.S. President Trump signed a law formally ending the minting of the one-cent coin, commonly known as the penny. Although the penny had effectively stopped being struck about a year prior, this legislative act marks its official discontinuation as a circulating currency. The legislation, referred to as the "Common Cent Act," will cease the production and issuance of the one-cent coin. The penny has held a significant place in American history, and its cessation represents a notable shift in monetary policy and economic considerations. While the signing signifies a formal end, the actual halt in production occurred some time ago, indicating a deliberate economic and administrative adjustment. This move may have implications for collectors, certain industries, and the public's daily transaction habits, though its symbolic significance may outweigh its immediate economic impact.

关注点 The discontinuation of the one-cent coin marks the end of a long-standing symbol in American currency. While the practical impact may be limited due to prior cessation of production, it reflects a shift in economic policy and potentially signals further changes in the future of U.S. coinage.
