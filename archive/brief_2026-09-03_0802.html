Opposition parties accuse Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez's administration of abandoning Ceuta's residents, while the government contends that right-wing parties are exploiting the situation for political gain. The crisis saw an unprecedented influx of at least 72,000 migrants from Morocco into Ceuta on July 30-31. While most have been returned, up to 5,000 remain, causing unrest. A police report suggesting Moroccan forces were "permissive" during the influx contradicts the government's stance, intensifying criticism. Protesters in Ceuta demand the migrants' return or redistribution, feeling neglected and their border security compromised. The government faces a difficult balancing act between humanitarian concerns, border security, and domestic political pressure. The incident also highlights the complex relationship between Spain and Morocco.

关注点 The protests in Spain over the Ceuta migrant crisis reveal deep societal divisions and political polarization regarding immigration and border security. The government's handling of the crisis has become a major political liability, potentially impacting its stability and future policy decisions on migration and relations with Morocco. It also underscores the broader European challenge of managing irregular migration flows from Africa.
