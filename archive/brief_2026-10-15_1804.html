Detailed English brief

This move, implemented on October 7, aims to alleviate a financial crisis and cash shortage triggered by disruptions to oil exports through the Strait of Hormuz, which account for approximately 90% of Iraq's government revenue due to the US-Iran war. However, the devaluation has created significant uncertainty and losses for Iraqi traders. As Iraqi regulations prohibit dollar-denominated trade, merchants must purchase goods with dinars, while imports are priced in dollars. The weaker dinar means traders need more local currency to acquire dollars, devaluing their dinar holdings and increasing procurement costs. Traders like Hamza al-Rudayni in Baghdad have reported substantial losses and have suspended trading activities, leading to a halt in market activity. Consumers face the prospect of higher prices for imported goods, and the government's ability to pay salaries may also be affected, exacerbating economic difficulties.

关注点 Iraq's currency devaluation is a significant economic policy shift with potential repercussions for its financial stability, trade, and the livelihoods of its citizens, particularly in the context of regional geopolitical tensions.

Analysts believe the bridge's opening will boost cross-border trade, particularly in areas like raw materials, daily necessities, and agricultural products. However, its overall impact on North Korea's economy remains to be seen due to the continued restrictions of international sanctions.
