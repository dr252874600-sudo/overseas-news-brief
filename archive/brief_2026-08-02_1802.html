Detailed English brief

Ahmet Davutoglu, former Prime Minister of Turkey and a key architect of its foreign policy, has announced that his opposition Future Party will cease political activities, effectively withdrawing him from party politics. Davutoglu, once a close associate of President Recep Tayyip Erdogan, rose to prominence with his "strategic depth" doctrine, which advocated for Turkey to leverage its geography and historical ties to become a central diplomatic power. During his tenure as Foreign Minister and later Prime Minister, Turkey expanded its influence, pursuing a policy of "zero problems with neighbors" and strengthening regional engagement. However, Davutoglu later became a critic of the political system he helped shape. His decision to step back from politics is framed as a move to distance himself from what he calls Turkey's "polluted political climate," rather than a surrender. His departure leaves a complex legacy, marked by his intellectual contributions to Turkish diplomacy but also by his struggle to translate that influence into an independent political movement.

关注点 Davutoglu's withdrawal from politics marks the end of a significant chapter in Turkish political history. His "strategic depth" doctrine profoundly influenced Turkey's foreign policy for years, and his departure signifies a potential shift in the country's political landscape and its intellectual discourse on foreign affairs. His critique of the "polluted political climate" also offers insight into the internal dynamics and challenges within Turkish politics.
